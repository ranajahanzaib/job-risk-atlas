# Scientific methodology and interpretation

The interface distinguishes two source systems. ORS estimates physical requirements and other job characteristics for its covered workforce (reference year 2023). CFOI enumerates fatal occupational injuries (reference year 2024). The datasets differ in coverage, collection, and time. No worker-level linkage exists here.

The primary CFOI rate unit is fatal occupational injuries per 100,000 full-time-equivalent workers. Prefer the BLS-published rate and its denominator metadata. Do not reconstruct a rate from an unrelated fatality count and employment estimate. A fatality count, a rate, a share, and an individual's probability are different quantities.

Occupation reconciliation must use reviewed occupational classifications and explicit match states. Names alone are insufficient. Parent occupational aggregates must never be assigned to detailed occupations. Event classifications preserve their published hierarchy; overlapping parent and child counts must not be added.

Body-region selection is a navigational illustration connecting activity labels to body areas. It makes no claim about anatomical load, strain, injury, or worker health. Occupational-level associations are ecological and do not establish causation.

For source definitions consult [ORS](https://www.bls.gov/ors/), [CFOI definitions](https://www.bls.gov/iif/definitions/census-of-fatal-occupational-injuries-definitions.htm), and [CFOI hours-based rates](https://www.bls.gov/iif/additional-resources/cfoi-hours-based-rates.htm).
